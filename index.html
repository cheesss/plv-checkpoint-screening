The question

A metric is only useful when its evidence matches the decision.

Action error measures imitation on expert states. Open-loop regularity measures temporal behavior without feedback. Replanning diagnostics ask whether a policy revises its future intent after the observation changes.

These are successively more specific questions. A learned model does not become a better selector merely because it closes a loop.

The control that matters

Accurate dynamics does not guarantee correct checkpoint ordering.

Under matched starts, old plans, execution stride, and diffusion seeds, simulator-induced replanning has an adverse oriented correlation of -0.212. The experiment separates transition fidelity, model-to-simulator agreement, and success ranking.

The resulting rule is deliberately conservative: temporal disagreement is evidence for targeted rollout, not automatic authority to reorder every checkpoint.

Practical conclusion

Screen competence cheaply. Diagnose temporal disagreement. Roll out the unresolved cases.

Action L1→Open-loop regularity→Replanning diagnostic→Targeted rollout

This is an evidence hierarchy, not a claim that every later stage is intrinsically better. PLV earns its role when action-chunking behavior and cheap metrics disagree.
